
# Potentiality

## Double Slit

Run the same **NineVolt** configuration three times and check the log. Three executions may produce **three different histories**, yet resolve to the **same object**. Two spectators watching the same execution may record different accounts. Hence, **a trace through the field follows only one route of many.** The log in the demonstration therefore exists solely to illustrate the computation.

Fundamentally, an execution and its computation are not necessarily the same thing. An observer encounters a projection, evaluates its local admissibility, and acts. The rule does not consult a clock or reconstruct how the projection arrived. **The Helix contributions can arrive in different admissible orders while producing the same result.**

The **double-slit experiment** raises questions about potential paths and what observation may reveal about them. When two spectators record different histories of the same computation, which account describes what actually happened?

***If geometry determines the result, what does the order of events actually prove?***

## Electron Cloud

An incoming **computational object** excites a socket whose observers already occupy a configuration. Each observer responds from its own position as **positive and negative packets** circulate through the field. Their encounters expose **a Boolean cloud of potential formations** without requiring any observer to represent the whole.

The **Hamming shells** organize these possibilities, while admissibility governs movement through the geometry. Different excitations can produce distinct arrangements and computational outcomes within the same underlying structure. The positions may change, but **every admitted transition must respect the geometry**.

A hydrogen atom offers a structural parallel. When excited, its electron may occupy a different orbital, changing the shape of its probability cloud. Similarly, the **Boolean cloud** can assume new formations as its observers respond to incoming packets. **The geometry constrains the possibilities, while excitation determines which relationships become active.**

***A resolved configuration may itself become the next excitation, extending the computation into another field.***

## Carbon Lattice

In diamonds, each carbon atom forms **four covalent bonds** with neighboring atoms, creating a **repeating tetrahedral lattice**. No individual atom represents the entire crystal. Its structure emerges from the relationships among them.

The Boolean cube also contains **two interlocking tetrahedra**, suggesting how small computational fields might compose into larger geometric structures. One resolved object could expose a continuation into another, with **charge, orientation, and admissibility** determining whether the connection belongs. Fields might extend, rotate, mirror, or fold back upon themselves.

**A chain extends computation from one field to the next. A lattice introduces constraints from multiple directions.** During the formation of a lattice, each new object may have to satisfy more than one neighboring face. Compatible configurations can extend it, while incompatible relationships prevent further growth at that connection. **The resulting geometry might reduce the need for explicit coordination, allowing increasingly complex computations to emerge through local admissibility.**

***And still, the question remains the same. What if the structure of a computation determines how it proceeds?***
