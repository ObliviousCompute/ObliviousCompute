# Potentiality

## Double Slit

Run the same **NineVolt** configuration three times and check the log. Three executions may produce **three different histories**, yet resolve to the object. Two spectators watching the same execution might record different accounts. The log in the demonstration exists solely to illustrate the computation.

Therefore, **an execution and its computation are not necessarily the same thing**. A trace describes **one route** through the field, not a required itinerary. An observer encounters a projection, evaluates its local admissibility, and acts. The rule does not consult a clock or reconstruct how the projection arrived. The Helix **contributions can arrive in different admissible orders while producing the same result.**

The **double-slit experiment** raises questions about potential paths and what observation may reveal about them. When two spectators record different histories of the same computation, which account describes what actually happened?

***If geometry determines the result, what does the order of events actually prove?***

## Electron Cloud

An incoming **computational object** excites a socket whose observers already occupy a configuration. Each observer responds from its own position as **positive and negative packets** circulate through the field. Their encounters expose a **Boolean cloud** of potential formations without requiring any observer to represent the whole.

The **Hamming shells** organize these possibilities, while admissibility governs movement through the geometry. Different excitations can produce different arrangements and computational outcomes within the same underlying structure. The positions may change, but every admitted transition must respect the geometry.

A hydrogen atom offers a fascinating parallel. When excited, its electron may occupy a different orbital, changing the shape of its probability cloud. Similarly, the **Boolean cloud** can assume different computational configurations as its observers respond to incoming packets. The geometry constrains the possibilities, while excitation determines which relationships become active.

A resolved configuration may itself become the next excitation, extending the computation into another field.

## Carbon Lattice

Carbon atoms in diamond form four covalent bonds in a repeating tetrahedral network. No single carbon atom contains the whole crystal. Its structure arises from the local bonds repeated across the lattice.

Now consider the computational possibility. One small NineVolt field receives a projection, admits what belongs, resolves a VOLT, and exposes a continuation. Another field receives that projection and applies its own admissibility. A **hanging Gate** could join them without requiring either field to represent the larger expression.

The same three-observer machinery might be reused to carry a computation through many successive fields. More observer groups could operate **laterally**, admitting and resolving different parts of an expression at once. Specialized sockets might implement familiar Boolean operations, while richer geometric admission rules could describe relationships that would otherwise require several conventional gates. Those compositions remain proposals to construct and test, not capabilities already established by the current three-observer specimen.

This is also where the machinery inside each box becomes less important. A server, embedded device, or quantum processor could supply a compatible classical `+` or `−` projection. Its interior need not resemble NineVolt. A quantum interface would still have to translate measurement or control information into those classical projections; doing so would not, by itself, make the field quantum.

The experiment to build next is modest: **connect two fields through an admissible Gate continuation**. Show that the first can supply the second, that the second can resolve independently, and that the result can become an input again.

***A small field can finish a computation. A composition of fields might become a language of machines.***
