# Potentiality

*The field is small. What it may become is not.*

## Double Slit

Run the same NineVolt configuration three times. The spectator may record three different histories: one Helix moves before the other, a Gate captures before or after a projection, and packets arrive in different orders. Yet the executions can resolve to the same **VOLT**. The log remembers every encounter. The observers need only their present states.

This is the first distinction between an execution and its computation. A trace describes **one route** through the field, not a required itinerary. An observer encounters a projection, evaluates its local admissibility, and acts. The rule does not consult a clock or reconstruct how the projection arrived. The receiver establishes the input; the Helix contributions can then take different admissible orders while producing the same result, as the NineVolt traces illustrate.

The double-slit experiment offers an evocative comparison between possible paths and observable outcomes, but not a physical equivalence. NineVolt exchanges classical messages. Its paths do not carry quantum amplitudes, and its deterministic resolutions are not interference patterns. What the demonstration makes visible is a simpler question: **how much of the history must the computation know?**

***The spectator remembers the path. The field does not require a script.***

## Electron Cloud

The three-bit Boolean cube has **eight vertices**, but NineVolt does not need any one observer to hold the entire cube. Three independent observers exchange positive and negative projections. Their arrangement into receiver and Helix roles defines a small **oblivious field**. Across the charge configurations, each input vertex has **three distinct possible resolved outcomes**.

The cloud is the set of relational possibilities, not a bag of stored alternatives. Each observer occupies its own position. What becomes admissible depends on the charges and geometry present at an encounter. Different socket arrangements expose different continuations, even when the underlying observers and vocabulary remain the same.

An electron cloud is a useful analogy for *potentiality*, but NineVolt is not assigning a quantum probability to each vertex. The useful resemblance is structural: **the present configuration constrains what can happen next**. In this language, the **Gate** is especially suggestive. Rather than treating a captured Gate only as an intermediate position, a future rule could let its exposed polarity become an interface to another field.

A completed VOLT need not be the end of an expression. It might also be the next projection.

## Diamond Lattice

Carbon atoms in diamond form four covalent bonds in a repeating tetrahedral network. No single carbon atom contains the whole crystal. Its structure arises from the local bonds repeated across the lattice.

Now consider the computational possibility. One small NineVolt field receives a projection, admits what belongs, resolves a VOLT, and exposes a continuation. Another field receives that projection and applies its own admissibility. A **hanging Gate** could join them without requiring either field to represent the larger expression.

The same three-observer machinery might be reused to carry a computation through many successive fields. More observer groups could operate **laterally**, admitting and resolving different parts of an expression at once. Specialized sockets might implement familiar Boolean operations, while richer geometric admission rules could describe relationships that would otherwise require several conventional gates. Those compositions remain proposals to construct and test, not capabilities already established by the current three-observer specimen.

This is also where the machinery inside each box becomes less important. A server, embedded device, or quantum processor could supply a compatible classical `+` or `−` projection. Its interior need not resemble NineVolt. A quantum interface would still have to translate measurement or control information into those classical projections; doing so would not, by itself, make the field quantum.

The experiment to build next is modest: **connect two fields through an admissible Gate continuation**. Show that the first can supply the second, that the second can resolve independently, and that the result can become an input again.

***A small field can finish a computation. A composition of fields might become a language of machines.***
