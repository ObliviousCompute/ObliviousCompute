# Appendix A — Definitions

## Purpose

This appendix establishes the fundamental vocabulary used throughout the theory. Each definition introduces a single concept without assuming any particular implementation.

## History

History is an account of the sequence of events believed to have produced a present state.

History is distinct from the evidence that survives from the past. Evidence may support a historical account without uniquely determining one.

### Example

*Imagine an ancient coin is unearthed. The coin unquestionably exists and is evidence originating from the past. By itself, however, the coin cannot uniquely determine the sequence of events that produced its present location.*

## Reality

Reality is the collection of all presently admissible states.

## Geometry

Geometry is the complete structure of admissible states together with every admissible transition between them.

## Observer

An observer is any participant capable of perceiving and evaluating a proposed state.

## Genesis

Genesis is the initial admissible state from which progression begins.

## Field

A field is the complete space of possible states that constitute a reality.

## State

A state is an observer's view of the field at a particular moment.

## Cell

A cell is the fundamental unit of progression within the field.

## Invariant

An invariant is a property preserved by every admissible transition.

## Admissibility

Admissibility is the determination of whether a proposed state or transition satisfies every required invariant.

## Transition

A transition is the progression from one admissible state to another.

## Event Horizon

An event horizon is the current boundary of a cell's admissible progression.

## Verification

Verification is the process of determining whether a proposed state or transition is admissible.

## Synchronization

Synchronization is the successful verification of another cell's present state.

## Equivocation

Equivocation is the proposal of multiple advancements from a single event horizon.
